    Glossary & flashcards

    Every term the catalog introduces, in plain language. Practise them as flashcards: cards you know come back after longer and longer gaps (1, 2, 4, 8, then 16 days), and cards you miss come back tomorrow. This is the Leitner system from L0-05.

    Flashcards

    Term

    Definition

    All terms

    AI winter
    A period when funding, interest and progress in AI fall sharply, usually after early promises go unmet. The main ones were in the mid-1970s and from the late 1980s to the early 1990s. Introduced in L0-03.
    Arithmetic coding
    A compression method that uses a model's probabilities to encode a message in close to the model's cross-entropy in bits per symbol. A better predictor gives a shorter encoding. Introduced in L1-03.
    Array
    An ordered collection of numbers that can be processed all at once. NumPy's main data type. Introduced in L0-06.
    Artificial general intelligence (AGI)
    A hypothetical AI system that matches or exceeds people across essentially all cognitive tasks. There is no agreed definition or test, and whether or when it could exist is debated. Introduced in L0-02.
    Artificial intelligence (AI)
    The broad field of making machines do tasks we'd call intelligent, such as recognizing speech, planning, deciding or creating. There is no single agreed definition. Introduced in L0-01.
    Artificial neuron
    A simple calculating unit, loosely inspired by brain cells, that combines its inputs into one output. Neural networks are built from many of them. Introduced in L0-03.
    Attention
    A mechanism that lets a model decide how much each part of its input should influence each part of its output. It's the core of the transformer. Introduced in L0-03.
    Automation bias
    The tendency of people to over-trust the output of an automated system and check it less carefully than they should. Introduced in L0-04.
    Autoregressive model
    A model that generates a sequence one piece at a time, each piece drawn from a distribution conditioned on everything generated so far. Language models work this way. Introduced in L1-02.
    Backpropagation
    The method used to train neural networks: it works out how much each parameter contributed to an error, so each can be adjusted to reduce it. Introduced in L0-03.
    Bayes' rule
    A formula for reversing a conditional probability: P(A | B) = P(B | A) P(A) / P(B). It updates a belief about A after seeing B. Introduced in L1-02.
    Bias
    In AI, systematic unfairness or skew in a system's outputs, often inherited from its training data. Introduced in L0-04.
    Bias (parameter)
    A learned constant added to a weighted sum, such as b in wx + b. Unrelated to the unfairness meaning of bias. Introduced in L1-04.
    Bigram
    A pair of neighbouring items, such as two words in a row. A bigram model predicts each word from only the one before it. Introduced in L0-01.
    Bit
    The unit of information when logarithms are taken base 2. One bit is the surprisal of an event with probability 1/2. Introduced in L1-03.
    Calibration
    How well a model's confidence matches its accuracy. A well-calibrated model that is "90% sure" is right about 90% of the time. Introduced in L0-04.
    Categorical distribution
    A probability distribution over a fixed, finite set of outcomes, such as the possible next tokens. Each outcome has its own probability, and they sum to 1. Introduced in L1-02.
    Central difference
    A numerical derivative that compares the function a small step on each side: (f(x + h) - f(x - h)) / 2h. It's much more accurate than a one-sided difference. Introduced in L1-04.
    Chain rule
    The calculus rule that the derivative of a composed function is the product of the derivatives of its steps. Backpropagation applies it through a whole model. Introduced in L1-04.
    Chain rule of probability
    The rule that a joint probability equals a product of conditionals: P(a, b, c) = P(a) P(b | a) P(c | a, b). It's how a language model scores a whole sequence. Introduced in L1-02.
    Chatbot
    A program that converses with people in text or speech. Introduced in L0-03.
    Computational graph
    A diagram of a calculation as a series of simple steps, each taking earlier values as inputs. Gradients flow backward along it. Introduced in L1-04.
    Conditional probability
    The probability of one event given that another has happened, written P(A | B). It equals P(A, B) / P(B). Introduced in L1-02.
    Confabulation
    Another word for hallucination, used in NIST's Generative AI Profile. Some researchers prefer it because "hallucination" wrongly suggests the system perceives things. Introduced in L0-04.
    Conventional software
    A program that follows step-by-step instructions written by people, rather than behaviour learned from data. Introduced in L0-01.
    Cosine similarity
    A measure of how closely two vectors point the same way: the dot product divided by both lengths. It runs from -1 (opposite) through 0 (unrelated) to 1 (same direction) and ignores length. Introduced in L1-01.
    Cross-entropy
    The average surprisal when outcomes come from one distribution but are scored with a model's probabilities. It's the loss language models are trained to lower. Introduced in L1-03.
    Cumulative distribution
    Running totals of probabilities in a fixed order. Sampling picks the first outcome whose running total passes a uniform random number. Introduced in L1-02.
    Deep learning
    Machine learning that uses neural networks with many layers. Introduced in L0-02.
    Dependency pinning
    Fixing the exact version of every package a project uses (for example numpy==2.4.6), so everyone gets the same behaviour. Introduced in L0-06.
    Derivative
    The rate at which a function's output changes as its input changes: the slope of its tangent line at a point. Introduced in L1-04.
    Deterministic
    Giving exactly the same output every time it runs with the same input. Introduced in L0-06.
    Dimension
    The number of entries in a vector. A vector with three numbers lives in three-dimensional space. Introduced in L1-01.
    Discriminative model
    A model that chooses between categories or predicts a value for an input, such as "spam or not spam". Contrast with a generative model. Introduced in L0-01.
    Distributional hypothesis
    The idea that words used in similar contexts tend to have similar meanings. It's why vectors learned from text put related words near each other. Introduced in L1-01.
    Docstring
    The descriptive text at the start of a Python file or function. In this catalog, every example's docstring ends with its expected output. Introduced in L0-06.
    Dot product
    Multiply two equal-length vectors entry by entry and add the results. It's large when the vectors point the same way and are long. Introduced in L1-01.
    Empirical distribution
    The distribution given by a dataset's own frequencies: each observed outcome gets probability equal to the fraction of times it appears. Introduced in L1-03.
    Entropy
    The average surprisal of a distribution: how unpredictable its outcomes are. It's zero for a certain outcome and largest for a uniform distribution. Introduced in L1-03.
    Entropy rate
    The average entropy of the next symbol given all previous ones, in the limit of long text. It measures how predictable a source such as English text is. Introduced in L1-03.
    Euclidean distance
    The straight-line distance between the tips of two vectors: the length of their difference. Introduced in L1-01.
    Expected value
    The probability-weighted average of a quantity: what you'd get on average over many draws. Introduced in L1-02.
    Expert system
    A program that captures a specialist's knowledge as many hand-written "if… then…" rules. Popular in the 1980s. Introduced in L0-03.
    Exploding gradient
    When gradients grow very large as they pass back through many steps, because many slopes larger than 1 multiply together. Training becomes unstable. Introduced in L1-04.
    Finite difference
    An estimate of a derivative from the change in a function's output over a small step in its input. Introduced in L1-04.
    Forward KL
    KL divergence measured from the data's distribution to the model's, D_KL(P || Q). Minimizing it is the same as maximum likelihood and tends to be mode-covering. Introduced in L1-03.
    Forward pass
    Running a calculation from inputs to output, keeping the intermediate values the backward pass will need. Introduced in L1-04.
    Forward-mode differentiation
    Computing derivatives by multiplying the chain rule's local derivatives from the input side. One pass gives the derivatives of every output with respect to one input. Introduced in L1-04.
    Foundation model
    A very large model trained on broad data that can be adapted to many different tasks. Introduced in L0-02.
    Generative AI
    AI whose main output is new content, such as text, images, audio, video or code. Today the term usually means deep learning models that create content. Introduced in L0-01.
    Generative model
    A model that learns the patterns of a kind of data well enough to produce new examples of it. Introduced in L0-01.
    Gibbs' inequality
    The result that cross-entropy is never smaller than entropy, with equality only when the model matches the true distribution. Equivalently, KL divergence is never negative. Introduced in L1-03.
    Gradient
    The vector of all partial derivatives of a function. It points in the direction of steepest increase. Introduced in L1-04.
    Gradient checking
    Testing a hand-written or automatic gradient by comparing it with a numerical estimate from finite differences. Introduced in L1-04.
    Gradient descent
    Training by repeatedly taking a small step in the direction opposite to the gradient of the loss. Introduced in L1-04.
    Graphics processing unit (GPU)
    A chip originally designed for graphics that performs many calculations in parallel, which makes it well suited to neural networks. Introduced in L0-03.
    Grounding
    Connecting a model's output to specific, checkable sources, so its claims can be verified. Introduced in L0-04.
    Gumbel-max trick
    A way to sample from a categorical distribution: add independent Gumbel-distributed noise to each log-probability and take the largest. The result is an exact sample. Introduced in L1-02.
    Hallucination
    When a generative model produces content that sounds plausible but is false or made up, such as an invented fact or citation. Introduced in L0-04.
    Held-out data
    Data kept out of training and used only to evaluate a model. Scores on it show how well the model generalizes. Introduced in L1-03.
    Human in the loop
    A design in which a person reviews, approves or corrects an AI system's output before it is used. Introduced in L0-04.
    Jacobian
    The matrix of partial derivatives of every output of a function with respect to every input. Introduced in L1-04.
    Joint probability
    The probability that several events all happen together, written P(A, B). Introduced in L1-02.
    KL divergence
    Kullback–Leibler divergence: cross-entropy minus entropy. It measures the extra surprisal from using a model's probabilities instead of the true ones, is never negative, and isn't symmetric. Introduced in L1-03.
    Language model
    A model trained to predict the next piece of text, given the text so far. Repeating that prediction produces new text. Introduced in L0-01.
    Large language model (LLM)
    A language model trained on an enormous amount of text, with billions of parameters. Chat assistants are built on them. Introduced in L0-01.
    Law of large numbers
    The result that the average of many independent draws gets close to the expected value as the number of draws grows. It's why frequencies from large samples approach the true probabilities. Introduced in L1-02.
    Layer
    One stage of a neural network, which transforms the output of the stage before it. Introduced in L0-02.
    Learning path
    A recommended route through the catalog's lessons for a particular role or goal. Introduced in L0-05.
    Leitner system
    A simple spaced-repetition method using boxes: remembered cards move up a box and are reviewed less often, forgotten cards go back to box 1. Introduced in L0-05.
    Likelihood
    The probability a model assigns to the data actually observed, viewed as a function of the model's parameters. Introduced in L1-03.
    Local derivative
    The derivative of one step of a computational graph with respect to its own inputs. The chain rule multiplies these together. Introduced in L1-04.
    Log-probability
    The logarithm of a probability. Sums of log-probabilities replace products of probabilities, which avoids underflow on long sequences. Introduced in L1-02.
    Logits
    The raw, unnormalized scores a model produces, one per possible outcome. Softmax turns them into probabilities. Introduced in L1-02.
    Loss function
    A number that measures how badly a model does on some data. Training adjusts parameters to make it smaller. Introduced in L1-03.
    Machine learning (ML)
    The part of AI in which systems learn their behaviour from data, instead of following rules written by people. Introduced in L0-01.
    Marginal probability
    The probability of one event on its own, found by adding up a joint probability over all values of the other events. Introduced in L1-02.
    Markov assumption
    The simplifying assumption that the next state depends only on the current state, not on the longer history. Introduced in L1-02.
    Markov chain
    A random process that moves between states using fixed transition probabilities that depend only on the current state. Introduced in L1-02.
    Matrix
    A rectangular grid of numbers arranged in rows and columns. A model's parameters are mostly stored in matrices. Introduced in L1-01.
    Matrix–vector product
    Multiplying a matrix by a vector: each entry of the result is the dot product of one row of the matrix with the vector. The matrix's column count must equal the vector's dimension. Introduced in L1-01.
    Maximum likelihood
    Choosing a model's parameters to give the training data the highest probability. It's the same as minimizing cross-entropy on that data. Introduced in L1-03.
    Memorization
    When a model reproduces parts of its training data word for word, instead of producing new combinations. Introduced in L0-03.
    MockLLM
    The catalog's scripted stand-in for a language model. It gives fixed, predictable replies, so examples run offline and repeatably. Introduced in L0-05.
    Mode-covering
    The tendency of a model trained with forward KL to spread probability over every region where the data has probability, even if that puts probability between the peaks. Introduced in L1-03.
    Mode-seeking
    The tendency of a model trained with reverse KL to settle on one region of high probability and ignore others. Introduced in L1-03.
    Model
    The result of training: a set of learned numbers, plus the calculation that uses them to turn inputs into outputs. Introduced in L0-01.
    N-gram model
    A model that predicts the next word or character from counts of the previous few (n minus 1) items. A bigram model is the simplest kind. Introduced in L0-03.
    Narrow AI
    AI built and validated for a particular range of tasks. Most researchers would describe every AI system in use today (as of November 2026) as narrow, though some argue the largest models show meaningful generality. Introduced in L0-02.
    Nat
    The unit of information when logarithms are natural (base e). One nat is about 1.443 bits. Introduced in L1-03.
    Negative log-likelihood
    Minus the log of the probability a model gives the data, often averaged per token. Lower is better, and averaged per token it's the same as cross-entropy on that data. Introduced in L1-03.
    Neural network
    A model built from many simple calculating units, connected in layers, whose connection strengths are learned from data. Introduced in L0-02.
    Norm
    The length of a vector: the square root of the sum of its squared entries (the Euclidean norm). Introduced in L1-01.
    NumPy
    A free Python package for fast arithmetic on arrays of numbers. Almost every AI project in Python uses it. Introduced in L0-06.
    One-hot vector
    A vector of zeros with a single 1 marking one category, such as the correct next token. Introduced in L1-04.
    Package
    A bundle of ready-made code that you install and use in your own programs. Introduced in L0-06.
    Parameter
    One of the numbers inside a model that is adjusted during training. Large language models have billions of them. Introduced in L0-02.
    Partial derivative
    The derivative of a function of several inputs with respect to one of them, holding the others fixed. Introduced in L1-04.
    Perplexity
    Two raised to the cross-entropy in bits (or e raised to it in nats). It's the number of equally likely choices that would be as surprising as the model is on average. Introduced in L1-03.
    Prerequisite
    A lesson you should read before another one, because the later lesson builds on it. Introduced in L0-05.
    Probability distribution
    A description of how likely each possible outcome is. The probabilities of all outcomes add up to 1. Introduced in L0-01.
    Prompt
    The instruction, question or other input you give a generative model. Introduced in L0-01.
    Prompt injection
    An attack in which text the model reads, such as a web page or email, contains instructions that hijack the model's behaviour. Introduced in L0-04.
    Proper scoring rule
    A way of scoring probabilistic forecasts in which, on average, honestly reporting your true beliefs scores best. Log loss is one. Introduced in L1-03.
    Python
    A popular, free programming language, and the program that runs code written in it. Every example in this catalog uses it. Introduced in L0-06.
    Random seed
    The starting number for a random-number generator. The same seed always produces the same sequence of "random" choices. Introduced in L0-06.
    Relative error
    The size of the difference between two values divided by their combined size. Unlike absolute error, it means the same thing for large and small values. Introduced in L1-04.
    Retrieval practice
    Learning by recalling information from memory, for example by answering quiz questions. It strengthens memory more than re-reading. Introduced in L0-05.
    Reverse KL
    KL divergence measured from the model's distribution to the data's, D_KL(Q || P). Minimizing it tends to be mode-seeking. Introduced in L1-03.
    Reverse-mode differentiation
    Computing derivatives by multiplying the chain rule's local derivatives from the output side. One pass gives the derivatives of one output, such as a loss, with respect to every input. Introduced in L1-04.
    Rule-based system
    A system whose behaviour comes from rules written by people, usually in an "if… then…" form. Introduced in L0-02.
    Sampling
    Drawing an outcome at random according to a probability distribution, so likely outcomes come up more often. Generative models sample to produce output. Introduced in L0-01.
    Scale
    The size of a model and its training: the number of parameters, the amount of data and the amount of computation. Introduced in L0-03.
    Self-supervised learning
    Learning in which the answers come from the data itself, such as predicting the next word of a text. It's how large language models are mostly trained. Introduced in L0-02.
    Shape
    The size of an array along each axis, such as (3, 4) for a matrix with 3 rows and 4 columns. Most array bugs are shape mismatches. Introduced in L1-01.
    Smoothing
    Techniques that give some probability to contexts or continuations an n-gram model has never seen, often by borrowing from shorter contexts. Introduced in L0-03.
    Softmax
    A function that turns a list of real-valued scores into a probability distribution by exponentiating each score and dividing by the total. Introduced in L1-02.
    Spaced repetition
    Reviewing material at increasing intervals over days and weeks, which makes it last much longer than the same study time in one session. Introduced in L0-05.
    Stationary distribution
    A distribution over a Markov chain's states that one more step of the chain leaves unchanged. Under mild conditions, the chain's long-run frequencies converge to it. Introduced in L1-02.
    Supervised learning
    Learning from examples that each come with the correct answer, such as emails labelled "spam" or "not spam". Introduced in L0-02.
    Surprisal
    The information in one outcome: minus the log of its probability. Rare outcomes have high surprisal; certain ones have zero. Introduced in L1-03.
    Symbolic AI
    AI built from hand-written symbols, rules and logic, rather than learned from data. Introduced in L0-02.
    Tangent line
    The straight line that just touches a curve at one point and has the same slope there. Introduced in L1-04.
    Terminal
    A text window where you type commands for the computer. Also called a command line or shell. Introduced in L0-06.
    TinyLM
    The small character-level transformer language model you build in Level 2. Later lessons use it as a real, if tiny, model. Introduced in L0-05.
    Token
    A piece of text that a language model reads and predicts: a whole word, part of a word or a single character, depending on the model. Introduced in L0-01.
    Training
    The process of adjusting a model's numbers, using examples, until it performs its task well. Introduced in L0-01.
    Training cutoff
    The date after which a model's training data stops. The model knows nothing later unless it's given newer information. Introduced in L0-04.
    Training data
    The examples a model learns from during training. Introduced in L0-01.
    Transformer
    A neural network design built around attention, introduced in 2017. As of November 2026, nearly all large language models are transformers. Introduced in L0-03.
    Transpose
    A matrix flipped over its diagonal, so its rows become columns. Written with a superscript T. Introduced in L1-01.
    Turing test
    A test proposed by Alan Turing in 1950: can a machine hold a text conversation that an interrogator can't tell apart from a person's? Introduced in L0-03.
    Typosquatting
    Publishing a malicious package under a name that looks like a popular one, hoping people mistype it. Introduced in L0-06.
    Underflow
    When a number is too close to zero for the computer to store, so it becomes exactly 0. Multiplying many small probabilities causes it. Introduced in L1-02.
    Uniform distribution
    A distribution in which every outcome (or every value in a range) is equally likely. Introduced in L1-02.
    Unit vector
    A vector with length 1. Dividing any non-zero vector by its norm gives a unit vector pointing the same way. Introduced in L1-01.
    Unsupervised learning
    Learning to find structure in data that has no answers attached, such as grouping similar items. Introduced in L0-02.
    Vanishing gradient
    When gradients shrink toward zero as they pass back through many steps, because many slopes smaller than 1 multiply together. Early layers then barely learn. Introduced in L1-04.
    Vector
    An ordered list of numbers, which you can also picture as an arrow from the origin. Models represent words, images and states as vectors. Introduced in L1-01.
    Virtual environment
    A private folder of Python packages for one project, so it can't affect, or be affected by, other projects. Introduced in L0-06.
